Lab Access, Architecture, and Security

Documentation date: 2026-11-08.

Status and scope

This historical record preserves user confirmations and findings from an earlier audit. The documentation date is separate from the audit date. Findings were not revalidated. At the time, the decision was to keep the learning lab without Docker or a sandbox. The document records risks and a proposed migration. No configuration, permission, account, service, or network changes were made. No installations, elevation attempts, or new security tests were performed.

Risks and interpretation limits

  • The primary historical risk was broad execution under a passwordless sudo account. Gateway network exposure, tool approvals, and Linux process privileges are independent controls.
  • With the sandbox off, written instructions cannot enforce the operating-system limits of the agent identity.
  • Modes 0700 and 0600 limit other ordinary identities and still allow access by processes running as the owner.
  • No new audit covered AWS, firewall, SSH, updates, devices, browsers, history, secrets, sockets, effective permissions, or full configuration.
  • The recorded audit did not execute as root. This work documented supplied evidence without elevation or out-of-scope writes.
  • Historical results describe that observation, and current conditions may differ.

Proposed dedicated Linux identity

Historical proposal, pending authorization and implementation when written. For later evidence, see Experiment 003.

  1. Separate the human administrator from the OpenClaw account. The proposed account has no sudo, sudo/docker/lxd memberships, or privileged sockets. Review equivalent and indirect privilege paths in an authorized phase.
  2. Give the account only the ownership and access needed for its workspace, configuration, and credentials. Keep secrets outside the workspace, including copies in backups or logs. Separate and control human maintenance access.
  3. Restrict accessible files and commands as additional defenses. Distinguish tool policies from enforced OS controls and verify actual isolation before claiming sandboxing.
  4. Run the service under the dedicated account with compatible hardening. Restrict writes and reads to required directories and evaluate compatibility before altering service configuration.
  5. Retain the loopback Gateway and SSH tunnel. A public port or proxy is unnecessary for this migration.

Removing sudo leaves other capabilities in place. The account can still access its own secrets, may have network access, and may gain capabilities through authenticated browser sessions. Stronger isolation requires explicit design and verification.

Proposed migration stages

Each stage was proposed for separate authorization. None was executed as part of this historical record.

  1. Agree on scope, required capabilities, boundaries, owner, and intervention window. Validate relevant conditions and identify dependencies on identity, memory, delegation, and tools.
  2. Prepare a restricted backup that preserves ownership and permissions. Define rollback and avoid copying secrets into the workspace, outputs, or documentation.
  3. Prepare the dedicated account with minimum access and separate administration. Plan ownership without indiscriminate transfers or inherited privileges.
  4. Transition service and required data with secure credential handling. Keep loopback and the tunnel. Apply compatible hardening gradually with recovery points.
  5. Run authorized continuity and boundary checks. Record evidence without secrets and preserve human recovery access.
  6. Accept changes after verifying continuity and controls. On failure, follow the rollback plan and document residual risk. Retire old access or resources only after confirming they are no longer needed and obtaining authorization.

The record contains no executable migration or elevation steps.
